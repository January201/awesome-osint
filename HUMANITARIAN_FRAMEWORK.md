# Humanitarian Family Reunification Framework
## 8-Phase OSINT Protocol for Locating Lost Family & Children in Conflict Zones

> **Mission:** Safely locate and verify identities of family members separated by war, displacement, or humanitarian crises.  
> **Core Principle:** "Do No Harm" — Every search must prioritize the safety and privacy of vulnerable subjects.

---

## 🎯 Phase 1 — Define the Objective

**Standard OSINT:** Due diligence, asset discovery.  
**Humanitarian Adaptation:** Confirming survival, location, and identity of lost relatives without exposing them to risk.

### Primary Objectives:
1.  **Survival Verification:** Confirming the subject is alive and safe.
2.  **Location Identification:** Determining current refugee camp, host family, or shelter location.
3.  **Identity Confirmation:** Verifying identity via photos, names, or biometrics without public exposure.
4.  **Connection Establishment:** Finding a safe communication channel (NGO intermediary, secure messaging).

### Writing the Objective (Examples):
*   ❌ **Vague:** "Find my son."
*   ✅ **Specific:** "Confirm if 'Ahmed Hassan', age 8, last seen at [Camp Name] on [Date], is currently registered with UNHCR or ICRC under that name or a variation, without triggering public alerts that could expose his location."

### Safety Check Before Starting:
*   [ ] Does searching this name put the subject at risk if discovered by hostile actors?
*   [ ] Is the goal reunification or intelligence gathering? (Must be reunification).
*   [ ] Have we contacted official intermediaries (Red Cross) first?

---

## 🗂️ Phase 2 — Collect Seed Identifiers

**Critical Difference:** In conflict zones, identifiers change. Names are transliterated differently; phones are lost; documents destroyed.

### Primary Identifiers to Collect:
*   **Full Name & Variations:** Native script, English transliteration, nicknames, tribal/clan names.
*   **Last Known Location:** Specific village, checkpoint, camp, or coordinate.
*   **Physical Description:** Scars, tattoos, clothing in last photo, height, hair color.
*   **Family Connections:** Parents' names, siblings, clan/tribe affiliations.
*   **Displacement Route:** Path taken (e.g., "Aleppo → Turkey → Greece").
*   **Digital Footprints:** Old phone numbers, email, social media handles (even if inactive).
*   **Document Numbers:** Passport, National ID, Refugee Case Number (if known).

### Secondary Identifiers (Emerging):
*   **Photo Metadata:** GPS coordinates, device type from old images.
*   **Voice Recordings:** For voice matching if available.
*   **Associated NGOs:** Names of aid organizations they contacted.

> **Rule:** Collect *every* variation of the name. A child might be registered under a father's name, a mother's maiden name, or a misspelled version.

---

## 🔍 Phase 3 — Run the Initial Search

**Sequence for Humanitarian Context:**

### Step 1: Official Registries (Tier 1 Sources)
*   **ICRC Missing Persons:** Search "Restoring Family Links" database.
*   **UNHCR Data:** If you have a case number, verify status.
*   **National Disaster/Refugee Portals:** Government-specific lists (e.g., Ukraine's "Find Your Own", Syria's civil registry).
*   **Hospital/ morgue lists:** (Sensitive, requires NGO access).

### Step 2: Specialized Missing Person Platforms
*   **LostChildren/People Finders:** Platforms like "Children of War" (Ukraine), "Syria Tracker".
*   **Facebook Groups:** Search specific community groups ("Syrians in Turkey", "Sudan Evacuation").
*   **Telegram Channels:** Local volunteer rescue channels often post lists before official databases.

### Step 3: Image Reverse Search (Visual ID)
*   **TinEye / Google Lens:** Upload last known photo. Look for matches in news articles, NGO reports, or crowd-sourced "found" albums.
*   **Caution:** Do *not* upload photos of minors to public engines if it risks identification by traffickers. Use NGO-intermediated tools if possible.

### Step 4: Communication Check
*   **PhoneInfoga:** Check if old number is active, ported, or disconnected.
*   **WhatsApp/Telegram:** Check if number still has an account/profile picture update.

### Step 5: Document Everything
*   Log every database searched, date, and result (even negative results). Negative results help narrow the search area.

## 🔄 Phase 4 — Pivot From Every Discovery

**The Pivot Loop for Refugees:**

| Starting Identifier | Pivot Target | Expected Finding |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Name** | Camp Registration Lists | New Location / Case Number |
| **Case Number** | NGO Database | Contact Info for Caseworker |
| **Old Phone** | WhatsApp Profile Pic | Current Photo / Status Update |
| **Photo** | Reverse Image Search | News Article / "Found" Post |
| **Location (Camp)** | Volunteer Telegram Channel | Witness Accounts / Transfer Logs |
| **Parent Name** | Obituary/Martyr Lists | Confirm status of accompanying adult |
| **Username** | Social Media (Sherlock) | New Account created in host country |

### Example Pivot Chain:
1.  **Start:** Name "Olena Kovalenko", last seen Kyiv.
2.  **Pivot 1:** Search Ukrainian "Find Your Own" portal → Found match in **Lviv Shelter #4**.
3.  **Pivot 2:** Shelter #4 Facebook Page → Post lists transferred children to **Poland**.
4.  **Pivot 3:** Polish "Children of War" database → Search Name + DOB → **Found: Registered in Warsaw**.
5.  **Pivot 4:** Contact ICRC Warsaw → **Reunification Initiated**.

> **Stop Condition:** Stop pivoting when you reach a verified guardian, official NGO caseworker, or confirmed safe location. Do not pivot to find "more info" once safety is established.

## 📊 Phase 5 — Apply the Source Hierarchy

**Tier Classification for Conflict Zones:**

*   **Tier 1 (Authoritative):** ICRC, UNHCR, Government Civil Registries, Official Camp Management. *(Verified Facts)*
*   **Tier 2 (Reliable):** Established NGOs (Save the Children, Doctors Without Borders), Verified Volunteer Networks. *(High Confidence)*
*   **Tier 3 (Unverified):** Facebook Groups, Telegram Channels, Crowdsourced Maps. *(Leads Only - Must Verify)*
*   **Tier 4 (Risky):** Anonymous forums, unverified social media accounts. *(Context Only - High Risk of Disinformation)*

> **Rule:** Never act on a Tier 3/4 lead without Tier 1/2 confirmation. Misinformation in war zones can lead families into traps.

---

## ✅ Phase 6 — Cross-Reference Findings

**What to Cross-Reference:**
*   **Name Spelling:** Does the spelling in the camp list match the passport? (Common issue with transliteration).
*   **Dates:** Does the arrival date at the camp match the witness testimony?
*   **Photos:** Does the child in the "found" photo match the birthmark/scars described?
*   **Locations:** Does the route make logical sense given the conflict timeline?

**Handling Conflicts:**
*   If two camps claim the same person → Contact both via official NGO channels immediately.
*   If a name appears on a "martyr" list but also a "refugee" list → Prioritize verifying life status via biometric/visual confirmation.

---

## 🎚️ Phase 7 — Assign Confidence Levels

*   **Low Confidence:** Single unverified social media post. *(Action: Do not contact family yet. Investigate further.)*
*   **Medium Confidence:** Match in two independent volunteer lists + matching photo. *(Action: Contact NGO intermediary to verify.)*
*   **High Confidence:** Verified in ICRC database + confirmed by caseworker + visual ID match. *(Action: Initiate reunification protocol.)*

---

## 📄 Phase 8 — Produce Structured Output

**Output Format: Family Reunification Dossier**

1.  **Subject Profile:** Verified Name, DOB, Physical Description, Last Known Location.
2.  **Current Status:** (Alive/Deceased/Unknown), Current Location (Camp/City), Custody Status.
3.  **Evidence Trail:** List of Tier 1 sources confirming status (Case Numbers, Dates).
4.  **Contact Protocol:** Safe method to make contact (e.g., "Contact ICRC Case Worker X, do NOT call direct number").
5.  **Negative Results:** List of places searched where the subject was *not* found (prevents duplicate work).
6.  **Risk Assessment:** Any security risks in the current location.

> **Critical:** This dossier is for **official use only** (NGOs, Family, Authorities). Never publish publicly.

---

## ⚠️ Legal & Ethical Boundaries

*   **GDPR / Privacy:** Do not process data of minors without legal guardian/NGO consent.
*   **Safety:** Do not reveal locations of safe houses or underground networks in public reports.
*   **Consent:** Ensure the subject (if old enough) consents to being found. Some may fear return.
*   **Trauma:** Approach with psychological sensitivity. Reunification can be traumatic.

**Tools used in this framework must be operated with these constraints in mind.**
